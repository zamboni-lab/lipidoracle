Stage 3: locating C=C and oxidation

MS2 matching tells you a lipid is PE 18:1_20:4. It does not tell you where on those chains the double bonds sit, or where a hydroxyl group has been added. Stage 3, acyl-chain analysis, answers that second question: it takes stage-2 results and reports idlevel 3 (all plausible positional candidates) and idlevel 4 (the per-feature consensus).

It runs on lipids that have already been identified, taking spectra acquired with a supported position-sensitive dissociation method and localising double-bond and, where supported, oxygen positions along each chain.

WORKFLOW:
  stage3: ead2   # False | ead1 | ead2 | uvpd | oad | ozid

This step is off by default. Select the engine matching the acquisition method: EAD, UVPD, OAD, or OzID. It cannot extract positional information from ordinary CID spectra.

The engines

The score_s3 column carries the localisation score, and score_s3_data and ms2_evidence record the supporting evidence. The engines are intensity-aware, but differ in their fragment-mass model, intensity transform, and eligible chemistry. Equal score_s3 values from different engines are not equivalent evidence and should not be compared across runs configured differently.

ead1: Fixed ladder, non-oxidised

ead1 implements the published method, described in full in Wu et al, Analyst, 2025, 10.1039/d5an00567a. For the experimental background, why EAD cleaves bonds that CID leaves intact and what the resulting fragment ladder looks like, see On the benefits and role of electron-induced dissociation in lipidomics.

It runs in four steps:

  1. Enumerate the hypotheses. For a chain of C carbons carrying n double bonds, every placement of those n bonds across positions 2 to C−1 is generated, excluding adjacent placements. Saturated chains, and chains under four carbons, yield a single trivial variant.
  2. Simulate the ladder. Each hypothesis is walked bond by bond from the methyl tail toward the carboxyl head. An ordinary step loses CH₂; a step crossing a double bond loses CH instead, and that is what shifts the ladder's spacing at the double bond. Every bond emits three neutral-loss variants, labelled 0, -H and +H (losses of CH₂, CH₃ and CH), weighted so the homolytic cleavage dominates.
  3. Weight by distance to the nearest double bond. Each step's weight is scaled by a hand-tuned array indexed on how many bonds separate it from the nearest double bond: 0.5 at the bond itself, 1 one bond away, 6 at two bonds, 1 at three, 0.5 at four and five, and 1 beyond. That spike at two bonds is where the positional signal actually lives. A bond flanked by two double bonds now takes max(left, right) of the two lookups rather than whichever happened to be evaluated last.
  4. Match and score. Predicted fragments from all chains are pooled and binned by m/z, with coincident masses summing their weights. Observed peaks are matched one-to-one against them within tolerance, by maximum bipartite matching, so no peak is counted twice.

ead1 does not support oxidised lipids at all. Oxidation parameters are ignored; chains with ;O are filtered out entirely. Use ead2 for anything oxidised.

ead2: Exact formulas, oxidised

ead2 takes the opposite approach: it computes exact elemental-formula losses for every cleavage, and scores each predicted fragment independently against the closest peak in tolerance rather than matching one-to-one. It supports hydroxyl and ketone oxygens natively, and resolves oxygen type the same way it resolves position — by spectral match.

Oxygen chemistry. A ;O suffix in a lipid name gives an oxygen count, not a chemistry. Since every hypothesis is anchored on a measured precursor, each candidate must be isobaric, so each oxygen group has to pay for itself:

Group Change Backbone cost
-OH hydroxyl +1 O none
=O ketone +1 O, −2 H one double bond

So 20:4;O admits 20:4;OH and 20:3;oxo, but never 20:4;oxo. An oxygenated carbon may not be sp2, so no double bond may land on or adjacent to it.

Two consequences worth stating plainly: the supported oxygen space is deliberately limited to hydroxyl and ketone readings, and hydroperoxide or extra-carboxyl interpretations are not enumerated at all.

Because the name only ever carries an oxygen count, ead2 must search both the all-hydroxyl and the ketone readings on every oxidised feature. On a mono-hydroxy reference set its top candidate lands on the wrong oxygen type in about 30% of spectra. That is the cost of not being told the chemistry up front, and it is inherent to the ;O data model rather than a scoring defect.

oad: MS-DIAL PC protonated profile

oad implements the pinned MS-DIAL PCOadSpectrumGenerator and common acyl OAD mass rules for one deliberately narrow profile: unoxidised diacyl PC [M+H]+, charge +1. Other classes, adducts, oxidised lipids, ether chains, and sphingoid bases are skipped rather than approximated.

For every proposed C=C position, OAD02 and OAD15 are essential anchors and both must be observed. OAD01, OAD03, OAD14, OAD16, OAD12+O, OAD12+O+H, and OAD12+O+2H are optional, intensity-weighted ranking evidence. Coincident theoretical masses are merged and observed peaks are assigned one-to-one.

OAD uses the same posterior, per-position marginals, distinct-rival resolvability report, and chemically impossible allene null as the other stage-3 engines. These quantify localisation uncertainty; they are not a discovery target-decoy q-value. The current posterior temperature was fitted on EAD reference sets, not OAD, so OAD probabilities remain provisional until a larger known-position OAD panel is calibrated.

ozid: paired aldehyde/Criegee products

ozid is a spectrum-level OzID engine, not a replacement for LipidOz's chromatographic XIC and isotope-envelope workflow. It enumerates non-adjacent C=C placements, converts each delta position to methyl-end notation, and reproduces LipidOz's exact precursor-formula aldehyde and Criegee products. PUFA products include the methyl-end double-bond index correction.

The initial validated profiles are PC/PE [M+H]+, PI [M+Na]+, and DG/TG [M+NH4]+, at charge +1. Molecular-species chain detail and a formula consistent with precursor m/z are required. Unlabelled lipids and the validated UltimateSPLASH global d5 formula are accepted; other labels, sum compositions, oxidation, ethers/plasmalogens, sphingoid chains, and other class/adduct combinations fail closed.

Each expected C=C produces an aldehyde/Criegee pair separated by one oxygen atom. Complete pairs dominate the mass-accuracy score; partial pairs remain in diagnostics but, by default, cannot establish localization. Identical theoretical evidence signatures are collapsed before posterior calculation, preventing chain-swapped or mass-degenerate structures from gaining probability merely by being enumerated more than once.

OzID preprocessing retains ions above the precursor and applies only ms2_noise_abs, not the global percentile filter ms2_noise_prc. A percentile threshold can otherwise discard the weaker member of a chemically required pair before formula-aware matching. Deuterated standards additionally require remove_d: False, and stage 3 still requires an eligible molecular-species parent from stage 2.

OzID uses the shared closed-set posterior and credible set, but its resolvability test is pair-specific: the winner needs more complete site-exclusive diagnostic pairs than its best structurally distinct rival. The optional allene null remains a diagnostic comparator. These are localization-confidence measures, not peptide-style target-decoy FDR or a claim of LipidOz-equivalent performance.

Choosing which candidates are reported

This is the second substantive change in v1.0.173. Selection is set by PARAM.s3_selection and is orthogonal to the engine — either method works with either engine, including on oxidised lipids.

Confidence tail reportsper-position marginal probabilitiesvote counts over the kept set

Why the change. score_s3 is normalised against each spectrum's own best candidate, so the top hit always reads 1.0 whether the evidence is overwhelming or absent. There was no way for the output to say this position is not determined. Thresholding on the score ratio inherits that blindness: two near-ties and two hundred near-ties both pass, and the number of survivors says nothing about how well localised the answer is. Measured against a null of chemically impossible (allene) placements — structures the enumerator excludes precisely because they cannot occur — an impossible candidate landed within 1% of the best real one in 11.4% of features. No value of the ratio cutoff separates real from impossible.

posterior instead converts the raw scores into a probability distribution with a softmax, then keeps the smallest set of top candidates whose probabilities sum to the credible level. The size of that set is the confidence statement: decisive evidence yields one candidate, an uninformative spectrum yields many. Positions are written into the idlevel-4 name only when a single placement carries at least s3_post_min_mass; otherwise the chain is reported at species level and the confidence tail still lists what is known.

The temperature is fitted, not chosen: swept against known positions on two reference sets, 0.01 maximises discrimination while keeping calibration error low. Larger values flatten the posterior towards chance, smaller ones manufacture confidence. Score gaps are taken relative to the top score, so one temperature is meaningful for both engines even though their raw scores differ by orders of magnitude.

The trade is coverage against correctness. Measured on a mono-hydroxy reference set with ead2, legacy asserts a C=C position on 84 of 96 features and is right on 55 (65%); posterior asserts 55 and is right on 40 (73%). legacy remains available for reproducing earlier results.

Configuration

Every stage-3 parameter is prefixed s3_ — after the stage that owns it, not the ID level it reports. The former ead_* and include_ms1_monochain spellings are still accepted, so existing configs load unchanged.

s3_max_candidates deserves particular care. A single oxidised token such as 20:4;O enumerates roughly 22,300 candidates. If the cap sits below that, the true structure can be stride-sampled out of consideration before scoring ever runs. The failure is silent and looks identical to a scoring failure in the output. The default of 10,000 suits non-oxidised chains; raise it to 30,000 for oxidised work.

How positions are reported

An idlevel-4 name carries a confidence tail listing each position and the support behind it, and the same numbers ride along in the CXSMILES trailer as dbPos(...) / mPos(...) tokens:

FA 20:4;OH [DB sn1: Δ5 100%, Δ8 100%, Δ12 100%, Δ14 50%, Δ15 50%; OH sn1: 11 100%]

Note where the hydroxyl position sits: in the tail, not in the name body. A group positioned mid-chain alongside unlocalised double bonds does not say how those bonds divide around it, so writing it into the body would make the name unrepresentable. See Nomenclature and CXSMILES for the full encoding.
