Locating C=C and oxidation by EAD

MS2 matching tells you a lipid is PE 18:1_20:4. It does not tell you where on those chains the double bonds sit, or where a hydroxyl group has been added. Acyl-chain analysis answers that second question.

It runs on lipids that have already been identified, taking their EAD spectra and localising double-bond and oxygen positions along each chain.

WORKFLOW:
  acyl_analysis: ead2   # False | ead1 | ead2 | uvpd

This step is off by default. It requires EAD spectra: it cannot extract positional information from CID or other dissociation methods. (v1/v2 are accepted as aliases of ead1/ead2.)

New in v1.0.173. Both engines were reworked, and candidate retention no longer depends on a score ratio. Three changes matter for anyone reading score_L3:

  • ead1 is now intensity-aware, which lifted top-1 positional accuracy from 28% to 80% on the arachidonic-acid reference set. Its old Pearson-correlation term is gone.
  • Candidate retention is posterior-based by default (l3_selection: posterior). The size of the reported candidate set is now itself the confidence statement.
  • Every parameter on this page was renamed ead_* → l3_*. The old spellings still load, so existing configs keep working.

The two engines

The score_L3 column carries the localisation score, and score_L3_data and ms2_evidence record the supporting evidence. Both engines are now intensity-aware — a candidate's raw score is Σ(weight × matched intensity) over the fragments it predicts — but they differ in the fragment-mass model, the intensity transform, and the candidate set the normalisation is estimated over. Equal score_L3 values from different engines are not equivalent evidence and should not be compared across runs configured differently.

ead1: Fixed ladder, non-oxidised

ead1 implements the published method, described in full in Wu et al, Analyst, 2025, 10.1039/d5an00567a. For the experimental background, why EAD cleaves bonds that CID leaves intact and what the resulting fragment ladder looks like, see On the benefits and role of electron-induced dissociation in lipidomics.

It runs in four steps:

  1. Enumerate the hypotheses. For a chain of C carbons carrying n double bonds, every placement of those n bonds across positions 2 to C−1 is generated, excluding adjacent placements. Saturated chains, and chains under four carbons, yield a single trivial variant.
  2. Simulate the ladder. Each hypothesis is walked bond by bond from the methyl tail toward the carboxyl head. An ordinary step loses CH₂; a step crossing a double bond loses CH instead, and that is what shifts the ladder's spacing at the double bond. Every bond emits three neutral-loss variants, labelled 0, -H and +H (losses of CH₂, CH₃ and CH), weighted so the homolytic cleavage dominates.
  3. Weight by distance to the nearest double bond. Each step's weight is scaled by a hand-tuned array indexed on how many bonds separate it from the nearest double bond: 0.5 at the bond itself, 1 one bond away, 6 at two bonds, 1 at three, 0.5 at four and five, and 1 beyond. That spike at two bonds is where the positional signal actually lives. A bond flanked by two double bonds now takes max(left, right) of the two lookups rather than whichever happened to be evaluated last.
  4. Match and score. Predicted fragments from all chains are pooled and binned by m/z, with coincident masses summing their weights. Observed peaks are matched one-to-one against them within tolerance, by maximum bipartite matching, so no peak is counted twice.

What changed in v1.0.173

ead1's score used to be matched weight / total predicted weight — the fraction of its own ladder that found any peak at all. Because the engine emits three neutral-loss variants at every bond, that ladder covers nearly every nominal mass in the window, so roughly 50 of 57 bins matched for every hypothesis alike. On a typical spectrum the entire spread across chemically unrelated structures was 0.880–0.890: the ranking inside that 1% band was noise, and a 0.99 relative cutoff kept all of it.

The score is now the raw sum Σ(weight × √intensity) over matched peaks, mapped back into [0,1] by the same log/MAD transform ead2 uses. The Pearson correlation term is gone, and l3_min_score now applies after normalisation, where an absolute floor on a 0–1 scale is meaningful.

Two extra gates decide whether idlevel 4 claims a position at all, on top of whichever selection method is in force:

  • l3_min_margin (0.05) — the relative gap (s₁ − s₂)/s₁ between the best two candidates, read on the raw scale, because normalisation pins the winner at 1.0 by construction.
  • l3_min_matched (3) — how many peaks the winner had to explain before that gap counts as evidence. A relative margin can look decisive when the whole comparison rests on one peak.

Below either gate the chain is named at species level (FA 20:4, not FA 20:4(5,8,11,14)) and the alternatives stay in the confidence tail.

Both gates were fitted against known positions carried in the reference spectra, not chosen by taste. The distance-weight array and its ×6 spike were kept: flattening either collapses accuracy to 11–20%, so the empirical claim the published method makes about EAD cleavage is load-bearing and correct — only its scoring was broken.

ead1 does not support oxidised lipids at all. Oxidation parameters are ignored; chains with ;O are filtered out entirely. Use ead2 for anything oxidised.

ead2: Exact formulas, oxidised

ead2 takes the opposite approach: it computes exact elemental-formula losses for every cleavage, and scores each predicted fragment independently against the closest peak in tolerance rather than matching one-to-one. It supports hydroxyl and ketone oxygens natively, and resolves oxygen type the same way it resolves position — by spectral match.

Oxygen chemistry. A ;O suffix in a lipid name gives an oxygen count, not a chemistry. Since every hypothesis is anchored on a measured precursor, each candidate must be isobaric, so each oxygen group has to pay for itself:

Group Change Backbone cost
-OH hydroxyl +1 O none
=O ketone +1 O, −2 H one double bond

So 20:4;O admits 20:4;OH and 20:3;oxo, but never 20:4;oxo. An oxygenated carbon may not be sp2, so no double bond may land on or adjacent to it.

Two consequences worth stating plainly: the supported oxygen space is deliberately limited to hydroxyl and ketone readings, and hydroperoxide or extra-carboxyl interpretations are not enumerated at all.

Because the name only ever carries an oxygen count, ead2 must search both the all-hydroxyl and the ketone readings on every oxidised feature. On a mono-hydroxy reference set its top candidate lands on the wrong oxygen type in about 30% of spectra. That is the cost of not being told the chemistry up front, and it is inherent to the ;O data model rather than a scoring defect.

Choosing which candidates are reported

This is the second substantive change in v1.0.173. Selection is set by PARAM.l3_selection and is orthogonal to the engine — either method works with either engine, including on oxidised lipids.

Why the change. score_L3 is normalised against each spectrum's own best candidate, so the top hit always reads 1.0 whether the evidence is overwhelming or absent. There was no way for the output to say this position is not determined. Thresholding on the score ratio inherits that blindness: two near-ties and two hundred near-ties both pass, and the number of survivors says nothing about how well localised the answer is. Measured against a null of chemically impossible (allene) placements — structures the enumerator excludes precisely because they cannot occur — an impossible candidate landed within 1% of the best real one in 11.4% of features. No value of the ratio cutoff separates real from impossible.

posterior instead converts the raw scores into a probability distribution with a softmax, then keeps the smallest set of top candidates whose probabilities sum to the credible level. The size of that set is the confidence statement: decisive evidence yields one candidate, an uninformative spectrum yields many. Positions are written into the idlevel-4 name only when a single placement carries at least l3_post_min_mass; otherwise the chain is reported at species level and the confidence tail still lists what is known.

The temperature is fitted, not chosen: swept against known positions on two reference sets, 0.01 maximises discrimination while keeping calibration error low. Larger values flatten the posterior towards chance, smaller ones manufacture confidence. Score gaps are taken relative to the top score, so one temperature is meaningful for both engines even though their raw scores differ by orders of magnitude.

The trade is coverage against correctness. Measured on a mono-hydroxy reference set with ead2, legacy asserts a C=C position on 84 of 96 features and is right on 55 (65%); posterior asserts 55 and is right on 40 (73%). legacy remains available for reproducing earlier results.

Configuration

Every idlevel-3/4 parameter is now prefixed l3_. The former ead_* and include_ms1_monochain spellings are still accepted, so existing configs load unchanged.

l3_max_candidates deserves particular care. A single oxidised token such as 20:4;O enumerates roughly 22,300 candidates. If the cap sits below that, the true structure can be stride-sampled out of consideration before scoring ever runs. The failure is silent and looks identical to a scoring failure in the output. The default of 10,000 suits non-oxidised chains; raise it to 30,000 for oxidised work.

How positions are reported

An idlevel-4 name carries a confidence tail listing each position and the support behind it, and the same numbers ride along in the CXSMILES trailer as dbPos(...) / mPos(...) tokens:

FA 20:4;OH [DB sn1: Δ5 100%, Δ8 100%, Δ12 100%, Δ14 50%, Δ15 50%; OH sn1: 11 100%]

Note where the hydroxyl position sits: in the tail, not in the name body. A group positioned mid-chain alongside unlocalised double bonds does not say how those bonds divide around it, so writing it into the body would make the name unrepresentable. See Nomenclature and CXSMILES for the full encoding.

Diagnostics

diag/annotation_idlevel3.csv lists the retained positional candidates and diag/annotation_idlevel4.csv the per-feature consensus. diag/l3_resolvability.csv is new in v1.0.173: it carries the posterior over candidates, the per-position marginals, and the margin and matched_n columns behind the idlevel-4 gates. It reports only; it gates nothing.

In the dashboard, the MS2 match tab shows the mirror plot for a selected candidate, and clicking a peak maps it onto the structure so you can see which cleavage supports a given position call.
